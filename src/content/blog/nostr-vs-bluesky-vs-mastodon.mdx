Three decentralized protocols are competing to replace the old centralized social media model. Mastodon (ActivityPub) has been around the longest. Bluesky (AT Protocol) has the fastest growth. Nostr has the most radical architecture. Each one makes different tradeoffs, and the right choice depends on what you actually want.

This is an honest comparison. I use all three. I have opinions. You might disagree. Good. Let's get into it.

## Round 1: Identity and Ownership

**Mastodon.** Your identity is tied to a server. Your full handle is @username@server.domain. If your server goes down or the admin shuts it off, you lose your account. You can migrate your followers to a new server, but it's a manual process and you lose your post history. Mastodon is decentralized in the sense that no single company controls the network. But your identity is still rented from whoever runs your server.

**Bluesky.** Your identity uses a domain-based handle (like @you.com). If you own the domain, you control the handle. If you use a handle like @you.bsky.social, Bluesky controls it. The AT Protocol is designed for full portability, you should be able to take your identity, followers, and data to any provider. In practice, most users are on the official Bluesky infrastructure, and full portability isn't fully tested at scale yet.

**Nostr.** Your identity is a cryptographic public key. It looks like npub1abc...def. No server controls it. No company owns it. You can move between any number of relays and clients without losing anything. Your identity is yours permanently, as long as you keep your private key safe.

**Winner: Nostr.** Key-based identity is the only model where no third party can take your account away. The tradeoff is that losing your private key means losing your identity permanently. There's no "forgot password" flow.

## Round 2: Ease of Use

**Mastodon.** This is Mastodon's biggest weakness. Before you can sign up, you have to pick a server. Most people don't know what a server is, let alone which one to choose. The official website lists hundreds of servers with different rules, topics, and moderation policies. Choice paralysis is real. After you pick one, you discover that some servers block other servers, so you might not be able to follow certain people.

**Bluesky.** This is Bluesky's biggest strength. Download the app, pick a username, you're in. No server choice. No key management. It feels like any other social app. The invite-only era is over, and onboarding is smooth.

**Nostr.** This is somewhere in the middle. You need to generate and back up a cryptographic key. That scares people. Nostr clients have gotten better at handling this, but it's still more friction than a username/password flow. Wisp helps by automating key generation and providing clear backup prompts, but the underlying complexity is still there.

**Winner: Bluesky.** For pure ease of getting started, Bluesky wins. Nostr has narrowed the gap with clients like Wisp, but it's not there yet.

## Round 3: Censorship Resistance

**Mastodon.** Your server admin can delete your account. They can also block entire other servers, preventing you from seeing content from those communities. The federation model distributes control, but it doesn't give you individual sovereignty.

**Bluesky.** Bluesky the company currently controls moderation. They can remove content and accounts. The AT Protocol is designed to support independent moderation services, but that's not fully operational yet. If Bluesky Legal decides your account violates terms, it can be removed.

**Nostr.** No one can delete your account. Your identity is a cryptographic key. Relays can choose not to carry your content, but they can't take your identity. You can always find a relay that will accept your posts. This is the only architecture where censorship is structurally impossible at the protocol level.

**Winner: Nostr.** It's not close. The other protocols are decentralized in their operations but still allow centralized account deletion. Nostr makes it impossible by design.

## Round 4: Community and Content

**Mastodon.** Strong communities organized around servers. Excellent for niche interests. The fediverse includes Pleroma, Pixelfed (photo sharing), PeerTube (video), and other ActivityPub implementations. The community culture is well established but can be insular.

**Bluesky.** Largest user base of the three at 40+ million. Good for reach and discovery. The starter packs feature makes building a feed easy. The community leans heavily toward tech, journalism, and progressive politics. Content discovery is strong thanks to custom feeds.

**Nostr.** Smallest user base at roughly 10,000 monthly active users. Strong in Bitcoin, open source, and censorship resistance circles. Less content overall, but the content that exists is higher signal-to-noise. Community is welcoming to newcomers, especially those using Wisp.

**Winner: Bluesky.** More users means more content, more conversations, more discovery. Nostr's community is great if you're in its niche, but it's not where you go for broad reach.

## Round 5: Monetization and Sustainability

**Mastodon.** No ads. Servers are funded by donations or membership fees. There's no native payment system. This keeps the platform clean but means servers can shut down when funding runs out.

**Bluesky.** Currently ad-free. But Bluesky is a venture-backed company that has raised significant funding. Venture capital eventually needs a return. The most likely path is ads or premium features. Whether that changes the character of the platform remains to be seen.

**Nostr.** No ads by design. Native Lightning Network zaps provide a peer-to-peer payment system for tipping creators. No company takes a cut. Relays can be free or paid. Clients like Wisp are open source and free.

**Winner: Nostr.** A protocol with built-in payments and no company needing a return is the most sustainable model long-term. Nostr doesn't need to "figure out monetization" because it's built in from day one.

## The Verdict

If you want the biggest audience and smoothest experience today, use Bluesky. It's a genuinely good product that improves on Twitter in meaningful ways.

If you want community governance and don't mind some friction, use Mastodon. The server model gives you real choice, even if that choice can be overwhelming.

If you want actual ownership of your identity and a platform that structurally cannot be taken from you, use Nostr. It's the only option where your account literally cannot be deleted by anyone else.

Most people will end up using more than one. There's no rule that says you have to pick one protocol. A Bluesky account for reach, a Mastodon account for community, and a Nostr account for ownership. They all work differently. They all have something to offer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is better: Nostr, Bluesky, or Mastodon?

Bluesky has the largest user base at over 40 million and smoothest onboarding. Mastodon has strong community governance. Nostr offers the strongest ownership guarantees. The best choice depends on your priorities.

### Can Nostr replace Twitter?

For the core social experience of posting and following, yes. But Nostr has roughly 10,000 monthly active users compared to Bluesky's 40 million. It works best for people who prioritize ownership over reach.

### Is Bluesky really decentralized?

Currently Bluesky is more centralized than its architecture promises. The AT Protocol supports decentralization but full portability and independent moderation have not been fully implemented.

### Which platform is easiest to set up?

Bluesky is the easiest. Mastodon requires choosing a server first. Nostr historically required understanding keys, but clients like Wisp now handle key generation automatically.

### Can I use more than one?

Yes. Many people use Bluesky for reach, Mastodon for specific communities, and Nostr for their permanent identity.
