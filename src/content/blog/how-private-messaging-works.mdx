Most messaging apps ask you to trust a company. Wisp asks you to trust math
instead. This post walks through exactly what happens when you tap send.

## What happens when you send a message

Your message never leaves your device as readable text. Wisp encrypts it
locally, wraps it so that even the metadata is obscured, and only then hands the
result to a relay.

The relay stores an opaque blob. It knows something was published, and roughly
when, but it cannot read the contents and cannot reliably tell who the recipient
is.

<Callout tone="info" title="On metadata">
  Encryption protects contents. Metadata — who talks to whom, and when — is a
  separate and harder problem. NIP-17 addresses a meaningful part of it, but no
  system removes metadata entirely.
</Callout>

## Where your keys live

Your private key is generated on your device and stored in the platform's secure
storage. It is never transmitted, never backed up to a server, and never visible
to us, because there is no "us" in the data path.

That has a sharp edge worth stating plainly: losing the key means losing the
account. Self-custody moves the responsibility to you, and that trade is the
whole point.

### Exporting your key

You can export your key at any time to move to another Nostr client. Wisp does
not lock you in, and the account you create here is not really "a Wisp account"
at all — it is a Nostr identity that happens to be used in Wisp.

## What a relay operator can see

A relay sees an encrypted event, a timestamp, and the network connection that
delivered it. It does not see your message text, and it cannot produce your
message text for anyone who asks.

Running your own relay removes even that. Most people will not, and that is a
reasonable choice — but the option existing at all is what makes the system
different.

## What this does not protect against

Encryption does not protect a device someone else is holding. If your phone is
unlocked and in another person's hands, no protocol helps you.

It also does not make anything you post publicly private. A public note is
public, permanently, on every relay that accepted it.
