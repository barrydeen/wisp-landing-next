One of Nostr's strengths is also one of its most confusing features for newcomers: there are a lot of clients. Because the protocol is open and anyone can build a client, the ecosystem has grown to include a dozen or more apps, each with a different philosophy.

Some are simple. Some are feature-packed. Some are web-based. Some are mobile-only. The right one depends on what you want to do. Here's an honest breakdown of every major Nostr client in 2026.

## What Makes a Good Nostr Client?

Before we compare, let's establish what matters.

**Onboarding experience.** Can a normal person set this up without reading a tutorial? Key generation, backup prompts, and initial feed setup should be smooth.

**Relay management.** Does the client handle relays for you, or do you need to understand what a relay is and find your own?

**Feed options.** Can you get a chronological feed? Are there optional algorithmic feeds?

**Feature set.** Does it support the NIPs you care about? DMs, zaps, long-form content, NIP-05 verification, communities?

**Platform support.** Is it available on your devices?

## The Contenders

### Wisp

Wisp is the newest major client, launching its 1.0.0 release on Google Play in mid-2026. It's built specifically to solve the onboarding problem.

**The good:** Normie Mode defaults to fiat currency, so Bitcoin-averse users never have to think about it. Key generation is automatic with clear backup prompts. Relay management is handled for you using intelligent routing. The UI is clean Material 3 design. Built-in podcast player. Protected chat rooms. Chronological feed by default with an optional For You feed.

**The not-so-good:** Android only (no iOS version yet). Smaller user base means fewer community features. Still actively developing, some features are on the roadmap rather than shipped.

**Best for:** Anyone new to Nostr. People who want a clean, no-hassle experience. Android users.

**Bottom line:** If you're on Android and you're new to Nostr, start here.

**Score:** 9/10 for new users, 7/10 for power users

### Damus

Damus is the original Nostr client for iOS, created by William Casarin. It's been around since Nostr's early days and has a loyal following.

**The good:** Established, stable, well-tested. Good DM support. Active development. Open source.

**The not-so-good:** iOS only. The UI is showing its age compared to newer clients. Onboarding is basic, you get a key pair but not much guidance. Relay management is manual. Some users have reported reliability issues with the latest versions.

**Best for:** iPhone users who want a tried-and-true Nostr client.

**Bottom line:** It works, but it's starting to feel dated.

**Score:** 7/10

### Amethyst

Amethyst is the most feature-rich Android client. If there's a NIP, Amethyst probably supports it.

**The good:** Incredible feature depth. Supports virtually every NIP. Highly customizable. Good for power users.

**The not-so-good:** Overwhelming for new users. The settings menu alone can be intimidating. Relay management is manual and complex. Performance can suffer with the feature bloat.

**Best for:** Power users who want maximum control and don't mind complexity.

**Bottom line:** The Android client for people who know what they're doing.

**Score:** 9/10 for power users, 4/10 for new users

### Primal

Primal is a polished client available on iOS and web. It has a controversial reputation in the Nostr community.

**The good:** Beautiful UI. Smooth performance. Good onboarding. Web version works great. Strong media support.

**The not-so-good:** Primal has been criticized for moving toward a more centralized model. The feed uses algorithmic ranking by default, which goes against Nostr's chronological ethos. Some users worry about the team's long-term plans for monetization.

**Best for:** People who want a beautiful, Instagram-like experience and don't mind a curated feed.

**Bottom line:** The best-looking Nostr client, but some philosophical tradeoffs.

**Score:** 8/10

### Snort

Snort is a web-based Nostr client aimed at power users.

**The good:** Fast, responsive, works in any browser. Good for desktop use. Feature-rich.

**The not-so-good:** Web-only. Less polished than native apps. Learning curve is steep.

**Best for:** Desktop users who want a browser-based Nostr experience.

**Bottom line:** A solid secondary client for desktop use.

**Score:** 7/10

### Iris

Iris is another web client with a clean, simple design.

**The good:** Simple interface. Easy to use. Good for light Nostr use.

**The not-so-good:** Limited features compared to Snort. Web-only. Development has slowed.

**Best for:** Quick, casual Nostr browsing on desktop.

**Bottom line:** Good for occasional use, but not a daily driver.

**Score:** 6/10

### Others Worth Mentioning

- **Coracle**, Web client focused on communities and groups.
- **Nostur**, iOS client with a unique UI approach.
- **Gossip**, Desktop client for power users who want full relay control.
- **Nozzle**, Minimalist Android client focused on speed.

## The Most Important Thing

Your Nostr identity works in every client. You can install Wisp on your phone, Snort on your desktop, and Primal on your tablet. They all access the same account, the same follows, the same posts. You are not locked into any client.

That's the point. On centralized platforms, the client IS the platform. On Nostr, the client is just a window into the protocol. If you don't like your window, change it. Your stuff stays.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Nostr client?

For Android users new to Nostr, Wisp is the best choice with Normie Mode and automatic relay management. For iOS, Damus and Primal are the main options. For power users, Amethyst offers the most features.

### Can I use multiple Nostr clients?

Yes. Your Nostr identity works across all clients. You can use Wisp on your phone and Snort on your desktop.

### Which Nostr client is easiest for beginners?

Wisp is designed specifically for beginners with automatic key generation, clear backup prompts, and intelligent relay routing.

### Is Primal safe to use?

Primal is polished but some have concerns about potential centralization. It is safe but privacy-focused users may prefer clients with chronological-only feeds.
