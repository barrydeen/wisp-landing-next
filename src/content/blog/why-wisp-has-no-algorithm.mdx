A ranked feed is not neutral. Someone chose what it optimises for, and that
choice shows up in what you see every day.

## What ranking actually optimises for

Ranking systems are trained against a measurable target — usually time spent, or
some proxy for it. The feed then becomes very good at producing that number.

The content that wins is the content that provokes a reaction. That is not the
same as the content you would have chosen, and over time the gap between the two
gets wide enough to notice.

## The case for chronological

A chronological feed has one rule, and you already understand it. Posts appear
in the order they were made. There is nothing to reverse-engineer and nothing to
game.

It also means the people you follow reach you. On a ranked feed, following
someone is a suggestion. Here it is an instruction.

<Callout tone="warn" title="The honest tradeoff">
  Chronological feeds get noisy if you follow a lot of very active accounts.
  That is a real cost, and the answer is better curation tools — not a ranker
  that quietly decides for you.
</Callout>

## What we build instead

Rather than ranking, we invest in controls: lists, mute rules, and follow
management that make it cheap to shape your own feed.

The design goal is that the feed is boring in the best sense — predictable, and
entirely a product of decisions you made on purpose.

## Where this could go wrong

If Wisp grows and the default feed becomes unusable for people following
thousands of accounts, we will have a genuine problem to solve. The commitment
is not that we will never build tooling for it. It is that the tooling will be
visible, optional, and under your control.
