Bluesky is good. Let's just get that out of the way. It's the best Twitter-like experience available right now for most people. The onboarding is smooth. The custom feeds are genuinely innovative. The user base is growing fast. If you left Twitter and landed on Bluesky, you made a fine choice.

But Bluesky is not the end of the story. There are things about it that should bother you more than they do, and there's a protocol that solves them. It's called Nostr, and a lot of Bluesky users are starting to use both.

## What Bluesky Gets Right

Let me give credit where it's due.

**Onboarding.** Download, pick a username, you're in. No server choice, no key management, no jargon. This is the bar every decentralized social app needs to meet. Bluesky's growth from zero to 40 million users in under two years proves that removing friction works.

**Custom feeds.** Bluesky lets you create and subscribe to algorithmically curated feeds. Want a feed of just cat photos? Build it. Want a feed of tech news from specific sources? Build it. This is genuinely powerful and something centralized platforms don't offer. The custom feed infrastructure has spawned an entire ecosystem of third-party feed providers.

**Starter packs.** The ability to share a curated list of accounts to follow makes onboarding for specific communities much easier. This is a smart feature that solves the cold-start problem new users face.

**Growth.** Bluesky crossed 40 million users in 2026 and is adding about 1.6 million per month. That's real network effect. More users means more content, more conversations, more reasons to check the app daily.

## Where Bluesky Falls Short

Here's the thing about Bluesky that I don't think enough people talk about.

**It's still a company.** Bluesky Public Benefit LLC controls the protocol, the main servers, the moderation system, and the app store listing. They've promised to decentralize, and the [AT Protocol](https://atproto.com/) is designed for that. But promises aren't architecture. As of 2026, Bluesky can still change the terms of service, ban accounts, and modify the feed ranking for all users. The company has raised over $30 million in venture funding, and that money comes with expectations.

**The moderation is centralized.** Bluesky sets the moderation rules. They enforce them. If Bluesky decides your account violates their rules, your account is gone. The AT Protocol technically allows you to port your identity elsewhere, but the ecosystem isn't mature enough for that to be a seamless experience yet. In practice, Bluesky accounts are still controlled by Bluesky.

**Monetization is coming.** Bluesky has raised venture capital. That money needs a return. The likely path is ads, premium subscriptions, or data licensing. None of those are inherently evil, but they all shift the incentives away from users. The question isn't whether Bluesky will monetize. It's how long they can delay it before investors demand results.

**The protocol is still controlled.** The AT Protocol is open source, but Bluesky the company maintains editorial control over its development. The roadmap is set by Bluesky, not by the community. This is a reasonable approach for a young protocol, but it's not the same as truly open governance.

## What Nostr Does Differently

**No company controls anything.** Nostr has no company behind it. There is no Nostr Inc. The protocol is maintained by the community through NIPs (Nostr Implementation Possibilities), which are proposed, debated, and adopted by client and relay developers. No single entity has veto power. If you don't like the direction of a particular client or relay, you switch to another one. Neither can change the protocol unilaterally.

**Your identity is truly yours.** If Bluesky bans your account, you can theoretically move your data to another provider. But that's a theoretical path through an ecosystem that doesn't fully exist yet. If Nostr bans your account, well, that can't happen. Your identity is a cryptographic key. No relay can take it from you. No company can delete it.

**No monetization pressure.** Nostr has no investors. No board. No revenue target. The protocol doesn't need to make money because no company owns it. Lightning zaps provide a peer-to-peer tipping mechanism, but there's no corporate entity extracting rent. Relays are run by volunteers, enthusiasts, and organizations that believe in the protocol.

**Client diversity.** Because the protocol is open, there are dozens of clients, each with a different philosophy. If you don't like how one client handles something, you switch to another. Your identity, follows, and posts come with you. This is fundamentally impossible on Bluesky, where the official client is the only full-featured option.

- Algorithm-free following of people you care about
